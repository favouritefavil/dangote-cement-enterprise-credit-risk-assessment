## 1. Executive Evidence Summary

The four annual reports are complete, audited, and structurally consistent year to year. Each presents **"Consolidated and separate"** primary statements throughout (income statement, other comprehensive income, financial position, changes in equity, cash flows), which directly satisfies the mandatory Group vs Standalone requirement (D003) — both bases are available in every year, as Direct Evidence.

The reports are unusually rich on debt, related-party, and parent-guarantee disclosure — richer than the original Data Requirements Matrix assumed might be available. Debt is explicitly and consistently described as **unsecured**. Parent-company (Dangote Industries Limited) guarantees over subsidiary loans are quantified in every year (FY2021–FY2025), showing a clear multi-year trend. Related-party balances and transactions are extensively disclosed with a ~20-entity narrative list.

The weakest areas are: no discrete note disclosing quantified, contracted forward capital commitments (DR025) — only narrative expansion disclosure was found; and no explicit disclosure of financial covenant ratios/terms on existing debt (DR023) beyond the fact that borrowings are unsecured. Several cross-year changes in subsidiary composition and ownership percentages were identified that affect comparability and require Project Lead attention before these figures are used in trend analysis.

## 2. Reporting Entity & Group Structure

**Direct Evidence** (consistent across all four years, Note 1 "General Information"):
- Dangote Cement Plc was incorporated in Nigeria on 4 November 1992, originally as Obajana Cement Plc; renamed 14 July 2010.
- Parent company: **Dangote Industries Limited ("DIL")**. Ultimate controlling party: **Aliko Dangote**.
- Registered address: 1 Alfred Rewane Road, Ikoyi, Lagos, Nigeria.
- "The Group" as used throughout these financial statements means Dangote Cement Plc and **its own subsidiaries only** — it does **not** include the wider Dangote Industries Limited conglomerate. DIL's own financials are outside the scope of these four reports.

*Report → Note 1, General Information → 2025 p.127 (materially identical wording in 2022 p.116, 2023 p.116, 2024 p.157).*

**Subsidiaries (Note 18):** Full table disclosed each year — subsidiary name, principal activity, country of incorporation, and % ownership (Group), plus a separate Company-level "Investments in subsidiaries" carrying-value table.
*Report → Note 18 → 2025 p.166–167; 2022 p.150–151 (materially equivalent notes exist in 2023 and 2024, not yet individually page-referenced).*

**Cross-year structural changes identified (see Section 12 for full list):** subsidiary count and ownership percentages are not static across FY2022–FY2025.

**Requires External Source:** Details of DIL itself (the ultimate parent's own financial condition) are not disclosed in these reports and would require DIL's own filings, which may not be public.

## 5. Qualitative Credit Information

**Direct Evidence, consistent across years:**
- Business model: cement/clinker production and distribution, Nigeria plus Pan-African operations.
- Footprint: "ten African countries" (2022–2024 cover language) → "11 African countries" (2025 cover language), reflecting the FY2025 commissioning of a Côte d'Ivoire grinding plant. *2025 p.15.*
- Strategic target: 80 million tonnes per annum capacity by 2030; current installed capacity 55Mta (35.3Mta Nigeria, 19.7Mta Pan-Africa) as at FY2025. *2025 p.15.*
- Key expansion projects named: 6Mta Itori Integrated Plant (in progress); planned doubling of Ethiopian plant capacity. *2025 p.15.*
- Governance: Board of Directors, Executive Committee, Board Committees (Audit/Risk, Finance & Investment, Sustainability & Technical, Remuneration/Governance/Nomination) disclosed each year in the Corporate Governance section.
- Top customers disclosed as a named list in supplementary information (e.g. 2025 "Our top customers," p.225) — **not yet reviewed in detail; flagged for follow-up.**

**Derived/Extractable:** Competitive position, market share — no direct disclosure found; would need to be inferred from volume/revenue trends plus external industry sources.

## 6. Debt, Liquidity & Financing Disclosures

**Direct Evidence — Note 26 "Financial liabilities" (consistent structure across all four years):**
- Instrument-level breakdown: bank loans, bonds, commercial paper, intercompany loan from Dangote Industries Limited, loan from Bulk Commodities Inc., by currency, nominal interest rate, and maturity.
- Explicit heading in every year: **"Unsecured borrowings at amortised cost."**
- Maturity profile table (Note 26.6): current portion split by <1 month / 1–3 months / 3–12 months, then year 2/3/4/5+.
- Example FY2025 instruments (Group): Loans from Parent Company ₦299.6bn (20.0% interest, due 2026); Loan from Bulk Commodities Inc. ₦73.4bn (USD, 6.0–8.5%); Commercial papers ₦209.6bn; Bond ₦198.3bn (11.85–23.5%, 2027–2034); long-term bank loans in USD, ZMW, CFA. *2025 p.174–176 (Note 26).*
- Intercompany loan from Dangote Industries Limited **first appears FY2024** (₦120.5bn); nil in FY2022/FY2023 — a new financing relationship, not a historical constant. *Cross-checked 2024 p.209, 2023 p.158, 2022 p.158.*

**Parent-company guarantees over subsidiary loans — Direct Evidence, quantified every year:**
| Year-end | Guaranteed amount |
|---|---|
| FY2021 | ₦87.0bn |
| FY2022 | ₦125.27bn |
| FY2023 | ₦258.08bn |
| FY2024 | ₦369.1bn |

## 7. FX / Currency Exposure

**Direct Evidence:**
- Functional/presentation currency is the Naira; foreign operations are translated at closing rate (balance sheet) and average rate (income statement), per policy Note 2.12. *2025 p.131–132 (consistent policy wording expected across years, not yet individually re-verified for 2022–2024).*
- **Currency translation reserve** (equity), a direct measure of cumulative FX translation effect on the Group's foreign operations:
  - FY2023: ₦625,160m
  - FY2024: ₦1,083,092m (Five-Year Summary, Group) / ₦1,083bn (Note 23.7, 2025 report's prior-year comparative)
  - FY2025: ₦1,003bn
  *2024 p.232 (Five-Year Financial Summary); 2025 p.173 (Note 23.7).*
- Debt is held in multiple currencies: Naira, USD, ZMW (Zambian Kwacha), CFA franc — disclosed instrument-by-instrument in Note 26.
- IAS 29 (hyperinflationary economies) is applied to certain subsidiaries and produces dual "inflation adjusted" vs "historical" figures — this interacts with FX translation and is a separate but related mechanism. *2025 p.193 (Note 35); 2024 p.230 (Note 35).*

Per the disposition governing this review, no conclusion is drawn here about whether FX is the dominant driver of credit performance — that determination is left for later analysis once the full evidence base is assembled. This section records only what is disclosed and its approximate scale.

**Requires External Source:** CBN Naira exchange-rate series, for independent verification of translation impact against actual currency movements.

## 12. Cross-Year Comparability Issues

These are material and should be reviewed before the figures are used in trend analysis:

1. **Subsidiary composition change:** Dangote Cement Ghana Limited (100% owned, FY2022) and Dangote Takoradi Cement Production Limited (referenced in the FY2024 IAS 29 note) both appear in earlier years but are **absent from the FY2025 Note 18 subsidiary table**, which lists only Dangote Cement (Sierra Leone) Limited among the IAS 29 hyperinflation entities. The reason (disposal, deconsolidation, reclassification) is not explained in the sections reviewed.
2. **Ownership dilution:** Dangote Cement Senegal S.A. ownership was 99.99% in FY2022 and FY2024, but **89.99% in FY2025** — a partial dilution not yet explained from sections reviewed.
3. **Entity naming:** "Dangote Mines Limited, Tanzania" (2022 Note 18) vs "Dangote Cement Limited, Tanzania" (2025 Note 18) — appears to be a renaming rather than a different entity, but not independently confirmed.
4. **New financing relationship:** The Dangote Industries Limited intercompany loan first appears in FY2024 financial liabilities; it did not exist in FY2022/FY2023 figures.
5. **IAS 29 hyperinflation scope changes year to year:** the set of subsidiaries reported on a hyperinflation-adjusted basis differs between FY2024 (Ethiopia, Sierra Leone, Ghana, Takoradi) and FY2025 (Sierra Leone only) — this affects how consolidated figures should be interpreted for trend purposes and is not a simple presentational constant.
6. **Country-footprint language:** "ten African countries" (2022–2024) vs "11 African countries" (2025) — explained by the Côte d'Ivoire grinding plant commissioning, but confirms the operating footprint is not static across the analysis period.

None of these have been resolved or assessed for materiality here — they are flagged for Project Lead review.
